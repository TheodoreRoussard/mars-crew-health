# Action guidance in this prototype

The interface starts each alert with something the crew member can do now: stop a risky task, verify a measurement, record the relevant context, protect a wound or follow an existing onboard procedure. Contacting the medical officer or flight surgeons remains an escalation step, not the entire action. No medication doses or new flight treatment protocols are supplied.

Temperature guidance distinguishes low readings from fever. Mass guidance distinguishes an increase from a loss; neither automatically prescribes extra food. Urine-strip guidance uses the existing hydration plan rather than an arbitrary extra litre. Sudden vision loss and severe pressure-change symptoms require immediate onboard help rather than waiting for an Earth reply.

These are illustrative hackathon prompts, not clinically validated spaceflight procedures. The spacecraft-specific wording (secure a resting position, task handover, suit isolation, approved onboard procedures) is a design adaptation and must be reviewed by flight medical and operations staff before operational use. The existing illustrative thresholds are unchanged.

## References checked on 15 November 2026

| Reference | Principle used in the action text |
| --- | --- |
| [NHS: fever in adults](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-adults/) | Rest, fluids and attention to worsening symptoms. |
| [NHS: dehydration](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/) | Small sips with nausea; seek help for concerning symptoms. |
| [NHS: dizziness](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/dizziness/) | Rest, slow movement and avoidance of hazardous activities. |
| [NHS: cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/) | Clean minor wounds with clean water, cover them and escalate suspected infection. |
| [NHS: vision loss](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vision-loss/) | Sudden loss of vision needs immediate assessment. |
| [St John Ambulance: hypothermia](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/hypothermia/) | Warm surroundings, dry insulation and medical help for hypothermia. |
| [NSW Health: hypothermia](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/environment/factsheets/Pages/hypothermia.aspx) | Avoid direct heat. |
| [American Heart Association: home blood pressure monitoring](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home) | Rest and correct measurement setup before a routine repeat; urgent symptoms require escalation. |
| [NASA: natural and induced environments](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-natural-and-induced-environments-vol-2/) | Radiation monitoring and sheltering are mission-level controls, not a clearance decision based on a single daily dose. |

Earth-based self-care references support only the general principles above; they do not validate the app's thresholds, diagnoses, timing, or use in microgravity. Dose records and test results are retained for review, and critical symptoms still lead to urgent crew assistance.
